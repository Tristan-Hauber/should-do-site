Should Do Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Summary

Should Do keeps your tasks on your device. The app has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it does not send your data to the developer or to anyone else.

What the app stores

  • Your tasks and settings. Task names, notes, dates, recurrence and completion history are saved in a file on your device, in storage shared only between Should Do and its own widgets.
  • Backups. If you use iCloud or iPhone backups, your device backup may include this file under your own Apple settings. The developer cannot see it. The app may also keep a recovery copy of the file if it ever has to repair it, and that copy is backed up in the same way.
  • Widgets. Widgets show task names on your Home Screen and, if you allow it, your Lock Screen. Anyone who can see your device may be able to read them. You control this in iOS settings.

Calendar access

If you allow it, Should Do reads your calendar events through Apple's calendar framework to work out when you are free. iOS describes this permission as "full access", but Should Do only reads: it never creates, changes or deletes calendar events. Event details are used only on your device to suggest tasks that fit your day, and are not stored or transmitted. A short summary of free time (start and end times only, no event names) is saved on your device so the widgets can use it. You can stop Should Do using your calendar at any time in Settings > Apple Calendar > Remove Calendar Integration, which also clears the saved free-time summary. To withdraw the permission itself, use iOS Settings > Privacy & Security > Calendars.

Deleting your data

Deleting Should Do from your device removes the app's stored tasks and settings from that device. Copies held in your own iCloud or device backups follow your Apple settings. The developer holds no copy to delete.

Sharing and third parties

Should Do does not share your data with third parties and contains no third-party analytics or advertising code.

TestFlight

If you install a test version through Apple's TestFlight, Apple may collect crash reports and usage data under its own terms and privacy notice. That data goes to Apple and the developer through TestFlight, not through the app.

Children

Should Do does not knowingly collect any personal information from anyone, including children.
